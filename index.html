About

About

Who am I?

I'm Shiva — a geospatial machine learning engineer in Albuquerque, working where remote sensing meets deep learning.

I started out building location search at consumer scale, where PostGIS queries had to return in 10 ms across 50,000 concurrent users or the product felt broken. That taught me to think in latency budgets before I ever thought about model architecture. Since then I've moved steadily toward the harder half of the problem — the models that decide what the geometry means.

Today that means classifying buildable terrain from raw DEM and shapefile data, writing CUDA-accelerated geoprocessing that took a 70 MW solar site from an overnight job to under twenty minutes, and maintaining the pipelines and evaluation harnesses that keep it honest at multi-terabyte scale. Before that, two years of spatio-temporal modelling across 15 public health studies — where the question was never just "is the model accurate" but "is it defensible."

I care about the unglamorous half on purpose. A segmentation model that's right 92% of the time and takes six hours isn't a product. The interesting constraint is almost never the architecture — it's throughput, latency, and whether anyone downstream can trust the output.

I'm most useful where the data is spatial, the volume is uncomfortable, and someone has to make a real decision from what comes out.
